## Claim C — Christopher Khokar (2025), self-described "intuitive, repeatable system"

Reported in a July 2025 news article (Greater Kashmir): Khokar, an independent Canadian researcher,
inventor, and entrepreneur with no formal linguistics degree, claims an "intuitive, repeatable system that
appears to successfully decode" the Indus script across seals, pottery, and artefacts, yielding "consistent,
logical interpretations" of the symbols. No claimed target language is specified in this source, no
method detail beyond that description, and no publication was found. He acknowledged the announcement's
timing was motivated by Tamil Nadu's $1M decipherment prize (offered by Chief Minister M.K. Stalin,
January 2025). **No independent verification, peer review, or scholarly response found.** Contextually
relevant: Steve Farmer (FSW 2004 co-author, already cited in this project's SQ-3 work) is reported in the
same search as saying in March 2025 that he and colleagues "are still sent pseudo-decipherments of the
symbols regularly," citing the prize specifically as a driver — useful context for why claim volume is
rising, not evidence about this claim's merits either way.

**Sourcing tier**: one live secondary news source (search-synthesis/WebSearch-summary tier, consistent
with this catalog's existing disclosure convention); the original article URL supplied by search was dead
(404), a working mirror found via a second search.

## Claim D — Steven Bonta, *A Partial Decipherment of the Indus Valley Script* (2023, 2nd ed. later)

A self-published, academic-site-hosted monograph proposing values for approximately 60 Indus signs,
arguing the predominant language of the inscriptions is an early form of Sanskrit or Indo-Aryan. Not
peer-reviewed. Distinct from both already-catalogued claims: unlike Yajnadevam's full-cryptogram approach
or the ResearchGate 185-Dravidian-reading claim, this one is explicitly scoped as "partial" (~60 of several
hundred signs) and targets Indo-Aryan/Sanskrit specifically, giving this catalog its first scoped-partial,
non-cryptogram-method claim.

**Sourcing tier**: search-synthesis tier only this cycle — the monograph itself was not directly fetched or
read.
