## Result

**"Semantic scope of Indus inscriptions comprising taxation, trade and craft licensing, commodity control
and access control: archaeological and script-internal evidence"** — Bahata Ansumali Mukhopadhyay,
*Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* 10(1), pp. 1-38, December 2023, DOI
10.1057/s41599-023-02320-7. **This is a real, peer-reviewed publication** (Palgrave Macmillan/Springer
Nature imprint), a materially different evidentiary tier from the self-published claims already on file —
though the author's own stated affiliation is corporate (Infor/Koch Industries), not an academic
institution or linguistics credential.

**The claim**: builds on the author's own 2019 paper (*Interrogating Indus inscriptions to unravel their
mechanisms of meaning conveyance*) arguing the inscriptions are **logographic/semasiographic, not
phonetic** — i.e., explicitly does not attempt to assign sound values to signs. The 2023 paper's own
abstract states the inscribed stamp-seals enforced rules on taxation, trade/craft control, commodity
control, and access control, and that two-sided tablets "were possibly trade/craft/commodity-specific
licenses issued to tax-collectors, traders, and artisans" (quoted directly from the abstract). The author
further argues the inscriptions encode no proper nouns (no personal/place/organization names), based on
combinatorial sign-pattern and geographic-distribution evidence.

**Reception, disclosed honestly**: not broadly accepted as settled — described elsewhere as a minority
view among the small set of scholars who favor an administrative-control reading. The Indus script remains
formally undeciphered regardless of this paper's own argument.

## Decision

Added to the Historian's catalog as a distinct category from the four already-filed phonetic-decipherment
claims: a peer-reviewed argument for the **non-linguistic/administrative** interpretation specifically.
This is directly relevant to SQ-3 (writing-system-type discriminant tests, itself still blocked on SQ-1's
corpus-rights question) in a way none of the other four catalogued claims are — worth flagging prominently
for whenever SQ-3 actually becomes executable, not filed as just one more item in an undifferentiated list.
