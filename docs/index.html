Open question #1 · Does this even encode language?

We are trying to understand the Indus script.
We don't yet know if it's writing.

Most inscriptions from the Indus Valley Civilization are tiny — commonly cited as averaging only about five signs, with the longest known example around seventeen. Whether this Bronze Age sign system encodes language at all, or is a non-linguistic notation for trade, clan, or ritual use, is a live, publicly disputed scholarly question — not a settled fact we're skipping past. Our first goal is to resolve, or honestly characterize, that question with falsifiable evidence. Our second is to document every step here. Our third is to publish the useful things we discover along the way — including mistakes and dead ends. This project just started: read on for what "started" actually means.

Why this is a harder starting line than either sibling project

The inscriptions are almost too short to test.

Individual Indus inscriptions are extremely short — commonly cited as averaging around five signs, with the longest known example only around seventeen — a corpus-wide property, not one unusual case. On top of that, scholars publicly and seriously dispute whether the script encodes language at all: a widely-discussed 2004 paper by Farmer, Sproat, and Witzel argued it does not, and that argument remains contested. This project treats that dispute as genuinely open, not a formality to wave past on the way to a translation attempt.
